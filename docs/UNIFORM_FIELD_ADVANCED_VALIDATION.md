**Uniform Electric Field — advanced enhancements and validation**

Validated locally on 18 October 2026. All five requested features are implemented in the existing Uniform Electric Field main tab.

1. **Files modified.** Updated `src/physics.js`, `src/presets.js`, `src/app.js`, `src/render.js`, `src/learning.js`, `src/styles.css` and `package.json`. Added `src/motion-state.js`, `src/motion-render.js`, `src/motion-ui.js`, `tests/motion-advanced.test.js`, `tests/browser/motion-advanced.spec.js`, `scripts/benchmark-motion.mjs`, `docs/motion-performance.json`, this report, and 14 reviewed screenshots under `docs/screenshots/uniform-field-advanced/`. The production build updates ignored `dist/` output. No dependencies were added. Point-charge constants and calculations, Electric Potential models/probes, and repository/deployment configuration were preserved.

2. **Reverse Polarity.** The dedicated button negates signed plate voltage without changing its magnitude, d, L, orientation, particle parameters, gravity or external force. Symbols, field, electric force, net acceleration, trajectory and calculations update together. The animation pauses and resets to t = 0 at a valid starting position; Play is required to restart. A geometry edit that would put the initial point on or through a plate moves that starting point back inside the gap. Reversal does not silently rebalance an external force.

3. **Acceleration vector.** Show Acceleration Vector is ON by default. A dashed amber arrow follows the particle beside the velocity/force arrows and uses the actual net `(aₓ,aᵧ)`. Its nominal scale is 6 CSS px per m/s², with a 70 px cap and a uniform reduction near viewport edges. The numeric values remain authoritative when the glyph is capped. No minimum arrow magnitude is invented. Zero net acceleration has no direction arrow and displays `a = 0 m/s²`. Field exit removes the electric contribution while gravity and the displayed external force continue. Stationary equilibrium retains its balancing force arrows. At an absorbing collision the stopped state has zero acceleration; the calculation explains contact force and impulse.

4. **Plate length.** A slider and exact input adjust L independently over 0.100–0.600 m. Separation remains independently adjustable over 0.005–0.200 m. Voltage remains independently adjustable over −2000–2000 V. Changing only L leaves `|E| = |ΔV|/d` unchanged. The actual open-end boundaries are at local tangential coordinates ±L/2, so length affects transit time, deflection, collisions and subsequent motion. Viewport scaling fits the plate geometry and starting point; length/orientation edits reset zoom to 1. A supplied length change never changes the physics merely to fit the drawing.

5. **Orientation design.** One engine uses tangent/normal unit vectors. Horizontal plates use tangent `(1,0)` and normal `(0,1)`; vertical plates use tangent `(0,−1)` and normal `(1,0)`. Switching rotates the initial position and velocity clockwise between equivalent arrangements. Horizontal signed voltage is `V_bottom − V_top`; vertical signed voltage is `V_left − V_right`. E equals signed voltage divided by d along the normal. Gravity remains `(0,−9.81)` m/s² in world coordinates. External force is explicitly controlled in world x/y coordinates and applies both inside and outside the field region.

6. **Four experiments.** Initially at rest keeps zero initial velocity. Perpendicular launch uses tangential entry speed, horizontal or vertical as appropriate. Parallel/reversal uses normal speed and supports slowing, momentary rest, reversal and impacts. Dynamic equilibrium retains gravity/electric balancing for the horizontal default. The vertical equilibrium preset visibly supplies `F_external = −(qE + mg)`; the force appears in the diagram, legend, settings and calculations. The Rebalance Forces button explicitly recomputes that force. Orientation changes in the equilibrium experiment explicitly adapt the displayed balancing force; polarity reversal leaves it fixed and can break equilibrium. Existing investigations and challenges remain available, with orientation-aware normal-component questions and an explanation that horizontal electric force alone cannot balance vertical weight. Reset restores the selected experiment's default particle/field parameters at the chosen L and orientation; Replay restores t = 0 with the current parameters and waits for Play.

7. **Automatic timer stop.** Animation and timer use the single authoritative simulated time `s.time`. The existing 0.1 simulated seconds per real second at 1× is preserved, with the existing 0.25×/1×/2× speed controls. Play/Pause, Step, Replay and Reset all use the same motion state. Full canvas exit, plate impact, stationary motion or the time safeguard stop playback and cancel the animation callback. Completed results ignore additional Step requests until replay/reset. The compact result area preserves actual final time, velocity, acceleration, event and trajectory, with a Replay button. It displays the requested exit/collision messages without a modal.

8. **Field exit versus canvas exit.** Field exit is a nonterminal boundary event. E becomes zero outside the rectangular ideal region, while gravity and external force continue. Motion and time continue while the particle is visible. Canvas exit is terminal only when the particle disk is fully outside: the center crosses a world boundary computed from the actual CSS canvas bounds expanded by the shared 9 CSS-pixel particle radius. Tangency to the expanded boundary counts as zero remaining visible disk area. The renderer and exit solver share this radius. Resize/zoom changes rendering boundaries, not physical parameters. New boundaries are checked from the current elapsed time, so resize cannot retroactively rewind the clock to an earlier hypothetical exit.

9. **Collision timing.** Each constant-acceleration segment solves quadratic/linear boundary equations analytically. Candidate collisions require contact with a plate within its finite length. Open-end crossings, entry/reentry, impacts and full canvas exits compete by earliest time. Physical collision wins an exact simultaneous plate-corner/open-end event. At impact, the point-particle center remains on the plate plane, the timer is set to the actual contact time, and the absorbing model sets velocity/acceleration to zero. Incident velocity is retained separately and reported. The 9 px rendering radius is a visibility convention, not an added physical particle size for collisions. No step-size approximation is used for these terminal times.

10. **Stationary and timeout safeguards.** Exactly zero velocity and zero net acceleration stop at their actual stationary state, ordinarily t = 0. A momentary turning point with nonzero acceleration remains active. Tiny nonzero velocities/forces are not classified as stationary. The configurable simulated-time limit is 30 s by default, adjustable from 1 to 3600 s in secondary settings. A moving particle reaching it stops with `Simulation time limit reached`, rather than a false physical-exit claim. Inputs and clocks reject nonfinite/invalid values. Only force-summation residuals within four machine epsilons of the largest component are normalized to zero, preserving the original equilibrium roundoff policy. Hidden scenes and tab changes pause playback; restarting clears the old wall-clock anchor. Event iteration also has a finite defensive cap.

15. **Limitations.** Rendering/performance results use desktop Chromium and emulated mobile viewports; physical Android hardware, Safari/WebKit and Firefox were not tested. The benchmark excludes full DOM calculation-panel updates and is not an end-to-end mobile frame-rate guarantee. Plates represent an ideal abrupt uniform-field region with no fringing. Collisions use an absorbing point-particle model. External balancing force remains a constant vector after field exit, so equilibrium can end when the electric contribution disappears. The time limit is a numerical/classroom safeguard, not a physical event. User zoom may deliberately place geometry outside the view; visibility stopping then uses the current view without changing physical elapsed history. At an open-end plane, the boundary belongs to the field only for an inward crossing; this defines the ideal discontinuity consistently.
