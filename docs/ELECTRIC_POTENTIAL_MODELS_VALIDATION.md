**Electric Potential enhancements — implementation and validation report**

Validated on 18 October 2026. Changes are local; no commit, push or deployment was performed.

1. **Files changed.** Modified `src/app.js`, `src/render.js`, `src/equipotential.js`, `src/equipotential-render.js`, `src/learning.js`, `src/styles.css` and `package.json`. Added `src/field-lines.js`, `src/parallel-plates.js`, `src/parallel-plates-render.js`, `src/potential-model-ui.js`, `tests/potential-models.test.js`, `tests/browser/potential-models.spec.js`, `scripts/benchmark-potential.mjs`, `docs/potential-performance.json`, this report, and representative screenshots under `docs/screenshots/potential-models/`. The build updates ignored `dist/` output. GitHub/Vercel configuration and the SI constants in `src/physics.js` were not changed.

2. **Electric field line overlay.** Electric Potential has a new checkbox, OFF by default. It displays continuous teal trajectories with arrowheads alongside violet contours. It is independent of the existing vector-at-A and vector-grid controls. The field-line integrator was extracted from Electric Field into a shared module; that tab retains its existing configuration. Potential uses eight source seeds on narrow canvases, twelve on wider canvases, small adaptive integration steps, and spacing suppression to prevent duplicate near-overlapping trajectories. Paths stop at the viewport, source exclusion boundaries, small fields or unresolved sharp turns. Sources and probes render above the lines.

3. **Parallel Plates mode.** A compact Field Model selector stays inside Electric Potential. Defaults are 200 V and 0.020 m, with a positive top plate and a negative bottom plate at 0 V. Controls allow 20–1000 V, 0.005–0.040 m separation, polarity reversal and horizontal/vertical plates. Field strength is derived, never an independent input. Both line layers have independent switches; field lines start OFF. Plate labels, voltage contours, the separation, and a 0.01 m scale appear in the canvas. Each model retains its own probes, attachments, parameters, zoom and layers when switching. Existing point-charge presets and the legacy uniform-potential example remain available.

4. **Calculation methods.** Point charges use the unchanged SI equations `V = Σkq/r` and `E = Σkq(r−r_source)/r³` through the existing immutable sampler. Shared RK4 integration solves `dr/ds = ±E/|E|`; arrowheads always follow physical `+E`, including inward arrows around negative sources. Single-source contours remain exact circles. Multiple-source contours use the existing triangulated scalar grid; when field lines are enabled, 8 px cells and safeguarded Newton edge-root refinement improve their geometry. Shared grid-edge roots and contour geometry are cached. Plates use an analytic linear potential and constant vector field, with `E = |ΔV_plates|/d` and `V(s) = V₊ − (V₊−V₋)s/d`. Potential, snapping, challenges and calculations use this same model.

5. **Perpendicular relationship.** Independent central differences of scalar potential provide numerical equipotential tangents. For all seven point presets, the normalized absolute dot product with E is below `10⁻⁷` at the tested nonzero-field locations; the gradient points opposite E. Generated path tangents align with E to absolute cosine above 0.99, and potential decreases in the arrow direction. Rendered multiple-source contour chords, outside 40 px source exclusions and away from small fields, have normalized dot error below 0.02 for at least 95% of samples and below 0.08 for every tested sample. These bounds account for finite straight chords. Plate intersections are exactly perpendicular analytically and in canvas stroke tests. Proper inter-path crossings were absent in all seven preset tests. Exact nulls have no assigned direction; singularities are rejected without softening.

6. **Probes, snapping and sliding.** Both modes use the existing A/B movement, projection, attachment, detachment, Same Potential button and display comparison. Plate contours are analytic straight segments and support both orientations. Tests cover independent attachments, tangential sliding, detachment suppression, leaving the capture band, reattachment and free motion with snapping OFF. Same Potential finds distinct points even when probes start at the same tangential coordinate. Parameter/polarity/orientation changes release plate attachments explicitly; they cannot retain stale contour references. Probe dragging stays inside the modeled plate region, while invalid exact coordinates are rejected. Badge placement avoids obscuring either plate.

7. **Zero work.** The original verified-zero policy and tolerances are preserved. Display zero still requires two current shared-level attachments, actual potential residual checks, and an actual difference within the display tolerance. Raw values are retained. Detachment invalidates verified equality immediately. The independent 200 V / 0.020 m test gives `E = 10,000 V/m`; moving +2 nC by 0.005 m along E gives `ΔV = −50 V`, `ΔU = −1.0×10⁻⁷ J` and `W_field = +1.0×10⁻⁷ J`. Distinct positions along one plate equipotential give zero difference, energy change and work. Existing tiny nonzero differences remain unclamped.

8. **Mobile responsiveness.** The model selector and visualization switches are below the canvas, preserving the original canvas position and sticky navigation. Touch targets are at least 44 px. Orientation remains under a secondary disclosure. Automated checks cover 320×568, 360×640, 390×844, 430×932, 768×1024, 1280×800 and 844×390 in light and dark themes. There is no horizontal page overflow at those sizes. Vertical contour labels run along their lines to avoid crowding narrow gaps. Probe badges and contour labels use collision-aware placement. Ordinary vertical page scrolling remains necessary on phones.

9. **Numerical tests.** `npm test`: **110 passed, 0 failed**. This includes all 78 existing tests and 32 new tests. New coverage includes seven charge arrangements, radial signs, dipole direction, independent superposition, numerical orthogonality, trajectory direction, crossings, source exclusions/nulls, caching, plate scaling/reversal/orientation, uniform contour spacing, signed energy/work, snapping/sliding/detachment/reattachment, invalid regions and separate state.

10. **Browser tests.** The complete `npm run test:browser` run passed **90 tests, 0 failed**, comprising 69 original and 21 new tests. The 21 new tests were rerun after the final plate label/badge refinements. Coverage includes actual canvas strokes, exact baseline restoration when the overlay is hidden, all seven presets, parameter changes, model/tab state, work signs, magnetic snapping, Same Potential, real CDP touch gestures, independent detachment/recapture, themes, sticky navigation, responsive controls and activity setup. Chromium was used; mobile gestures/viewports are emulated.

11. **Screenshot review.** The new tests capture 42 screenshots: seven sizes × two themes × point overlay/horizontal plates/vertical plates. Representative screenshots were visually inspected at every requested size. Review led to moving controls below the canvas, moving separation text away from probes, using vertical contour labels in narrow vertical gaps, and preventing probe badge backgrounds from cutting across plates. Final retained examples are in `docs/screenshots/potential-models/`. Field arrows visibly follow the curves and cross violet contours perpendicularly; uniform plate lines remain straight and equally spaced. Earlier full-document capture timeouts were resolved by capturing the requested viewport, matching the existing browser suite's screenshot strategy.

12. **Build and integration.** `npm run build` succeeds and produces the static application in `dist/`. `git diff --check` passes. The four main tabs remain intact. Existing force, field contribution, potential, verified-zero, particle-motion, navigation, theme, investigation and challenge regressions pass. Added model-specific plate challenges use actual plate measurements rather than hidden point-charge system energy. The new guided experiment supplies point/plate setup buttons, predictions, hints and a matriculation-level explanation. No `SP025` label was added to the student interface.

13. **Performance observations.** `node scripts/benchmark-potential.mjs` measures rendering at a 390×844 emulated viewport, DPR 2, 364×371 CSS-pixel canvas. Median cached redraws are 0–0.1 ms. Median changed-parameter render times were 5.8 ms for a single charge, 14.7 ms for a dipole, 22.0 ms for four charges and 2.0 ms for plates; respective p95 values were 8.8, 42.7, 37.9 and 7.4 ms. These are desktop Chromium measurements, not physical Android results or end-to-end interaction latency. Full raw measurements are in `docs/potential-performance.json`. Background layers, trajectory paths and contours are cached. Field paths are not computed when hidden. The app schedules drawings on changes rather than continuously rendering an idle scene.

14. **Limitations and unverified behavior.** Fringing, finite-plate edge fields and all external-region measurements are deliberately outside the plate model. Probes are constrained to its central region; no exact exterior field or potential is invented. Trajectory density illustrates direction and is not a charge-density scale; paths are finite and stop near sources, nulls and viewport edges. Sampled point contours remain numerical approximations. The optional single-intersection 90° marker was not added. Actual Android hardware, Safari/WebKit and Firefox were not tested; sustained slow-device frame rates are unverified, and measured recomputation p95 can exceed a 16.7 ms frame. Highly zoomed objects can leave the visible canvas, as in the original application. No deployment was attempted.
